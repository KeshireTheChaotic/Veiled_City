# VEILED CITY — Campaign Frame
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE**

## Pitch
It is the present day. Magic did not disappear; the world learned not to see it.

The Veil bends attention and explanation around supernatural events. A thing with antlers can cross an interstate at midnight and become, by morning, a confused report about a deer. A haunting captured on six phones becomes compression artifacts. A ritual murder becomes gang violence. The Veil is powerful, but it is not perfect—and lately it has begun to fray.

Your character lives in both worlds. You may investigate supernatural crimes, collect dangerous debts, negotiate with inhuman powers, protect ordinary people, exploit hidden markets, or simply try to keep your rent paid while something ancient knows your phone number.

## Tone
Urban fantasy, occult mystery, modern noir, supernatural action, dark bargains, found family, morally complicated institutions, strange beauty, and moments of ordinary life under extraordinary pressure.

The default campaign does **not** assume grimdark play. Humor, romance, hope, community, and mundane life are important because they give the hidden world something meaningful to threaten and something worth protecting.

## Setting truths
1. The supernatural is real, but public consensus rejects it.
2. The Veil obscures; it does not make evidence impossible.
3. Technology works normally unless a specific supernatural effect interferes with it.
4. No supernatural faction is globally sovereign.
5. Names, promises, hospitality, ownership, thresholds, blood, memory, and attention can acquire magical weight.
6. Monsters can be people, people can be monsters, and neither category guarantees morality.
7. Powerful magic is possible, but preparation, leverage, relationships, and consequences matter.
8. Ordinary institutions remain powerful. Police, courts, hospitals, banks, landlords, corporations, journalists, networks, and governments do not vanish because magic exists.

## The hidden world you may know about
- **The Concord:** mediators and precedent-keepers for supernatural disputes.
- **The Lantern Office:** decentralized investigators and containment specialists.
- **The Glass Market:** a mobile occult marketplace where almost anything can be bought if you accept the correct currency.
- **The Choir Below:** entities associated with infrastructure, dead frequencies, tunnels, power systems, and communication networks.
- **The Pale Court:** immortal predators who adapted old aristocratic habits to finance, nightlife, property, and private security.
- **The Green:** old intelligences of rivers, storms, parks, vacant lots, animals, invasive species, and places cities never fully tamed.
- **Aster Vale Risk:** publicly an elite risk consultancy. People in the occult community disagree about how much it knows.
- **The Civic Anomaly Unit:** a rumored mortal investigative program that may or may not officially exist.

## Campaign questions
During Session Zero answer at least five:
- What first convinced you that the supernatural was real?
- What ordinary part of your life are you unwilling to surrender?
- Who taught you the first rule of the hidden world?
- Which faction already has an opinion about you?
- Who would be endangered if your double life became public?
- What supernatural debt do you owe?
- What did you see that you still cannot explain?
- What line will you not cross, even for power?
- What mundane skill keeps saving your life?
- Which place in the city feels like yours?
- What rumor about the hidden world do you desperately hope is false?
- Who knows your real name in more ways than one?

## Veil Exposure
The campaign tracks local **Veil Exposure** from 0–6. It measures how close your current web of supernatural events is to undeniable public recognition.

0 Quiet • 1 Ripples • 2 Pattern • 3 Breach • 4 Containment • 5 Public Incident • 6 Rupture

Exposure rises because of conspicuous magic, persistent evidence, mass witnesses, Fear consequences, or deliberate revelation. It can fall through containment, explanation, bargains, time, or successful efforts to redirect attention. Exposure is not a morality meter.

## What play looks like
Most sessions alternate between investigation/social scenes, ordinary-life scenes, dangerous supernatural scenes, and consequences. Combat is important when violence matters, but many threats are better solved through leverage, research, deals, escape, containment, or changing the conditions of a confrontation.

# VEILED CITY — Player Setting Guide
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE**

The modern world is real: rent, jobs, families, elections, traffic, hospitals, apps, cameras, insurance, bureaucracy, firearms, social media, and ordinary crime. The supernatural exists inside that world rather than replacing it.

## The Veil
The Veil is a pressure toward normal explanations. It can distort attention, encourage disbelief, blur some memories, and make supernatural patterns difficult to preserve. It is not perfect invisibility and it does not automatically delete recordings.

People can learn the truth. Some already have.

## Magic
Magic is intent given structure through symbols, relationships, practice, and metaphysical rules. Traditions vary wildly, but several principles recur:
- Similar things can influence one another.
- Names and identities create leverage.
- Thresholds distinguish one state or ownership from another.
- Promises become stronger when deliberately witnessed.
- Attention itself can feed some phenomena.
- Places remember repeated human behavior.
- Technology can host magic without being “anti-magic.”

A prepared ritual can often accomplish something a split-second spell cannot.

## Factions commonly known to the Awakened
### The Concord
Neutral tables, mediators, contract witnesses, and precedent keepers. It has influence because enemies still need somewhere to negotiate.

### The Lantern Office
Local cells investigate supernatural incidents, rescue people, and contain disasters. Cells vary in philosophy and professionalism.

### The Glass Market
A moving occult market appearing in liminal modern spaces: abandoned malls, hotel conference rooms, disused platforms, parking structures, encrypted channels, and dreams.

### The Choir Below
Spirits or emergent intelligences associated with infrastructure and communication systems. Scholars disagree whether the Choir is ancient, modern, or both.

### The Pale Court
Immortal predatory houses that converted feudal habits into property portfolios, clubs, finance, private security, and influence networks.

### The Green
A convenient label for local nature powers: rivers, storms, old trees, animals, vacant lots, parks, invasive species, and land that never consented to becoming urban.

### Aster Vale Risk
An elite private risk-consulting company rumored to employ occult specialists.

### Civic Anomaly Unit
A whispered mortal program that treats the supernatural as an evidentiary problem. Some Awakened insist it is real; others think the rumor is deliberate misdirection.

## Hidden-world law
There is no universal supernatural criminal code. Law is layered: mundane law, faction rules, local supernatural precedent, private bargains, ancient obligations, and raw power. Something being “legal” to a court does not make it legal to the city, ethical, or safe.

## Life beneath the Veil
Most Awakened people still have ordinary lives. They hold jobs, raise children, take medication, argue with landlords, file taxes, lose passwords, and get stuck in traffic. The hidden world is dangerous partly because it intrudes on things worth keeping.

# Character Creation in Veiled City
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE**

Use normal Daggerheart character creation unless this file says otherwise.

## 1. Choose a Daggerheart class
Any SRD 2.0 class can be used. Treat the class as a mechanical archetype rather than a medieval occupation.

Suggested modern interpretations:

| Class | Veiled City interpretations |
|---|---|
| Bard | performer, negotiator, influencer, confidence artist, occult advocate |
| Druid | urban animist, shapechanger, Green emissary, storm-worker |
| Guardian | bodyguard, monster-hunter, protective occultist, tactical responder |
| Ranger | investigator, tracker, hunter, wilderness/urban scout |
| Rogue | infiltrator, fixer, thief, intelligence operative |
| Seraph | miracle worker, sacred protector, chosen emissary |
| Sorcerer | innate caster, elemental talent, unstable magical prodigy |
| Warrior | detective, veteran, enforcer, martial specialist |
| Wizard | ritualist, researcher, occult academic, forensic magician |
| Assassin | covert operative, supernatural hunter, sanctioned killer |
| Brawler | street fighter, bruiser, kinetic adept, monster wrestler |
| Warlock | patron-bound agent, bargain mage, emissary |
| Witch | curse-worker, hedge magician, spirit-worker, practitioner |

## 2. Choose ancestry and community
You may use official SRD options or Veiled City options from `CARDS/ANCESTRIES_AND_COMMUNITIES.md`.

Ancestry is physical/metaphysical lineage. Community is how and where you were shaped socially. Do not treat a community as ethnicity.

## 3. Veiled City domain access
The four homebrew domains are **Hex, Signal, Veil, and Pact**.

At character creation, you may replace **one** of your class's normal domains with one Veiled City domain, with GM approval. That replacement is permanent unless a major campaign event explicitly changes it. The class still uses its normal Spellcast Trait.

Recommended thematic replacements rather than hard restrictions:
- **Hex:** ritualists, witches, occult scholars, curse-breakers, hunters.
- **Signal:** investigators, technomancers, information brokers, urban spirits specialists.
- **Veil:** infiltrators, illusionists, protectors, spies, social manipulators.
- **Pact:** patron-bound characters, mediums, spirit negotiators, oathkeepers.

The AI GM must not require a custom domain. Official domains work normally in Veiled City.

## 4. Experiences
Modern Experiences are encouraged. Examples: EMT, Corporate Security, Criminal Law, Local Reporter, Amateur Radio, Network Engineer, Urban Explorer, Bartender, Crime Scene Tech, Street Medic, Parish Volunteer, Rideshare Driver, Grad Student, Locksmith, Union Electrician, Social Worker, Firefighter, Archivist, Private Investigator.

Experiences should be broad enough to matter but narrow enough to say something about your character.

## 5. Starting gear
Choose normal starting gear or use Tier 1 options from `PLAYER/MODERN_EQUIPMENT.md`. Modern mundane objects that are reasonable for your background do not require inventory optimization unless they become mechanically important.

## 6. Establish three anchors
Write down:
- **Home:** where you can normally rest.
- **Person:** someone you care about who is not merely an adventuring resource.
- **Obligation:** job, family duty, debt, promise, custody, education, community role, or another reason you cannot simply disappear into the occult world.

These are player-owned story hooks. The GM may threaten or complicate them but should not constantly use them as hostages.

## 7. Choose an opening status
Pick one:
- Newly Awakened
- Born Behind the Veil
- Professional Investigator
- Indebted
- Survivor
- Hunter
- Emissary
- Accidental Witness
- Occult Criminal
- Trying to Get Out

This informs how much hidden-world lore your character begins knowing.

# Veiled City Player Quick Reference
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE**

## Core campaign meters
- **Hope / Fear:** normal Daggerheart Duality mechanics.
- **Veil Exposure 0–6:** how publicly undeniable the current supernatural situation has become.
- **Faction Standing:** descriptive unless a mystery or faction file gives it a specific clock.
- **Debts & Bargains:** explicit promises with named parties and terms; never let the GM invent a hidden obligation after the fact unless the bargain itself was knowingly uncertain.

## Hidden-world etiquette
These are customs, not universal laws:
- Do not give a supernatural being your full name casually.
- Hospitality can acquire magical force if both sides knowingly invoke it.
- A promise is more dangerous when witnessed, signed, named, or sealed with a meaningful object.
- Crossing a privately owned threshold may matter to some entities.
- Payment in the Glass Market can be symbolic as well as monetary.
- Never assume a spirit's metaphor is merely metaphor.

## Ordinary technology
Phones, cars, cameras, GPS, cloud services, firearms, public records, hospitals, and the internet work normally unless a specific effect says otherwise.

## Investigation principle
A failed roll should not erase the only route to an essential conclusion. Expect failure to change danger, cost, timing, attention, or clarity.

# Faction Primer
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE**

Your character may know all, some, or none of these at campaign start depending on their background.

- **The Concord:** neutral mediators and supernatural precedent-keepers.
- **The Lantern Office:** investigators and containment specialists with local cells.
- **The Glass Market:** moving occult marketplace with strict customs and dangerous debt.
- **The Choir Below:** spirits and intelligences tied to infrastructure, signals, and networks.
- **The Pale Court:** old predatory houses with modern property, money, and influence.
- **The Green:** local powers of land, water, weather, wildlife, parks, and neglected places.
- **Aster Vale Risk:** elite corporate risk consultancy rumored to know more than it should.
- **Civic Anomaly Unit:** possibly real mortal investigators linking impossible cases across agencies.

No faction is automatically “the good side.” Individual members can differ sharply from institutional policy.

# Vehicles in Veiled City
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE • PLAYTEST HOMEBREW**

Modern transportation matters in Veiled City, but vehicles should remain part of the fiction rather than becoming a second combat game. Use normal Daggerheart action rolls and environments for chases, escapes, collisions, and supernatural pursuit.

## Vehicle rule
A vehicle has **Tags**, not a separate pool of HP. When a consequence materially damages it, the GM gives it one condition:

- **Impaired:** one relevant function is unreliable; rolls depending on it may have disadvantage.
- **Disabled:** the vehicle cannot continue its current mode of travel until repaired or circumstances change.
- **Destroyed:** it is no longer a usable vehicle and may create an immediate hazard.

A vehicle normally moves from Fine → Impaired → Disabled → Destroyed, but a Severe collision, powerful supernatural attack, or obvious fiction can skip a step. Armor Slots on the PC do not automatically protect the vehicle.

## Common vehicles
| Vehicle | Useful tags | Typical strength | Typical weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compact Car | Agile, Common, Efficient | parking, traffic, anonymity | poor off-road performance |
| Sedan | Common, Comfortable, Stable | everyday travel, blending in | unremarkable acceleration |
| Motorcycle | Fast, Narrow, Exposed | splitting traffic, pursuit | rider exposed; poor cargo |
| Pickup Truck | Rugged, Cargo, Tow | barriers, hauling, rough roads | conspicuous in tight urban spaces |
| Cargo Van | Cargo, Concealable Interior, Heavy | teams, gear, mobile workspace | slow and easy to identify |
| SUV | Rugged, Passenger, All-Weather | mixed terrain and protection | bulky; mediocre concealment |
| Performance Car | Very Fast, Agile, Loud | pursuit and escape | attention, limited cargo |
| Utility / Emergency Vehicle | Heavy, Authorized-looking, Specialized | access and scene control | highly identifiable |

The tags are permissions and complications, not bonuses that stack automatically. Award advantage only when a tag creates a clear situational edge.

## Occult vehicle upgrades
A vehicle can normally support one major occult upgrade without special preparation; additional upgrades attract attention, cost, or maintenance.

- **Warded Cabin:** once per rest, gain advantage on a reaction roll made by an occupant against a magical intrusion originating outside the vehicle.
- **Saltline Channels:** during a moment of preparation, pour salt into built-in channels to make the closed cabin count as a crude protective circle until a door/window is opened or the line is broken.
- **Ghostglass Mirrors:** reflected supernatural creatures cannot hide solely by being invisible; they may still conceal themselves by other means.
- **Cold-Iron Frame Pins:** creatures with an established cold-iron weakness have disadvantage when attempting to physically possess or merge with the vehicle.
- **Signal-Ghost Transponder:** once per rest, spend a Hope to make ordinary digital tracking lose the vehicle for a few minutes. Supernatural surveillance may resist.
- **Hidden Compartment:** advantage when concealing an object small enough to fit from a routine mundane search. A targeted forensic search can still find it.
- **Threshold Key:** a prepared charm lets the vehicle cross one previously established supernatural threshold that would normally reject it. The key is consumed.
- **Emergency Circle Kit:** deploys a temporary Very Close ward around a stopped vehicle after several minutes of work.

## Chase resolution
Use an appropriate Veiled City environment and resolve a chase as a sequence of meaningful obstacles rather than a fixed number of driving checks.

A typical chase has three beats:
1. **Gain or lose position** — traffic, terrain, shortcuts, supernatural movement.
2. **Complication** — witnesses, road closure, collateral danger, occult interference.
3. **Decision point** — escape, intercept, abandon the vehicle, force a confrontation, or accept a cost.

Use **Agility** for raw control and reflexes, **Finesse** for precision, **Instinct** for reading traffic or pursuit, **Knowledge** for navigation/technical tricks, and another trait when the fiction genuinely supports it.

Hope should create openings, shortcuts, clean escapes, or protected bystanders. Fear should complicate the route, increase Veil Exposure, damage the vehicle, separate allies, attract authorities, or improve the pursuer's position rather than merely dealing damage every time.

# Occult Services & the Glass Market
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE**

The Glass Market rarely uses fixed prices. These are common service categories the PC may seek.

| Service | Common price forms | Typical complication |
|---|---|---|
| Curse diagnosis | cash, favor, minor secret | diagnosis identifies an enemy who notices the inquiry |
| Name research | secret, access, rare record | researcher wants reciprocal information |
| Spirit mediation | offering, service, apology | spirit insists on being heard rather than “solved” |
| Supernatural medical care | money, blood, future favor | treatment leaves a visible occult signature |
| Memory repair | treasured memory, confession, favor | repaired memory includes something the PC preferred forgotten |
| Veil cleanup | money, evidence, service | someone keeps a copy |
| Warded safehouse | favor, introduction | strict house rules |
| Relic appraisal | percentage of value, story, debt | appraiser recognizes provenance |
| Smuggling through a threshold | money, name, favor | route belongs to someone |
| Exorcism | service, sacred obligation | entity may have a legitimate claim |
| Information brokerage | information of equal value | truth may implicate an ally |
| Contract review | money, favor, future referral | lawyer refuses illegal metaphysical terms |

The GM should state the price **before** the player commits whenever the seller is honestly bargaining. Hidden traps are appropriate only when the fiction establishes deception or unknown supernatural terms.

# Veiled City — Case Hooks
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE**

These are spoiler-free starting premises. The GM may introduce them when appropriate rather than presenting them as a quest menu.

## The 3:17 Call — Tier 1
Disconnected phones across the city begin ringing at 3:17 AM. Anyone who answers hears their own voice calmly describing something that has not happened yet.

## Rent Due in Blood — Tier 1
Tenants in an aging apartment building are receiving eviction notices for violations they do not remember committing. One notice is addressed to a woman who died in the building thirty years ago.

## Dead Letter Office — Tier 1
Undeliverable mail begins appearing in occupied homes: letters written by missing people, dead relatives, and strangers who seem to know private details.

## Saint of the Red Line — Tier 2
A figure appears on train platforms seconds before serious accidents. Some witnesses call it a guardian angel. Transit officials call it a dangerous mass hallucination. Someone has begun trying to trap it.

## House Without Address — Tier 2
A house appears on navigation apps but not on the street. Deliveries are signed for. Property taxes are paid. Nobody can agree on where the house physically is.

## The Perfect Forecast — Tier 3
A private risk firm quietly begins preventing disasters before anyone else knows they are coming. Their success rate is impossible—and some of the disasters may only be happening because the company predicted them.

## Night of Open Eyes — Tier 4
The Veil begins failing district by district. For one night, thousands of ordinary people see the hidden city exactly as it is, while every major faction races to decide what the public will remember in the morning.

# Veiled City Ancestries & Communities
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE • PLAYTEST HOMEBREW**

Ancestry represents lineage and metaphysical/physical nature. Community represents upbringing and social context. These options are intentionally broad enough for a modern city.

## Ancestries

### Awakened Human
A human whose perception has permanently opened to the hidden world.
- **Second Sight:** Once per scene, ask whether one nearby phenomenon is supernatural, magically concealed, possessed, or altered. The GM answers truthfully, but need not explain the cause.
- **Adaptable:** When a relevant mundane Experience, institution, or piece of ordinary technology gives you a credible edge against a supernatural problem, you may mark a Stress to gain advantage.

### Changeling
Human enough to pass beneath the Veil; otherworldly enough that it never quite settles around you.
- **Borrowed Glamour:** Spend a Hope to subtly alter your visible features, voice, scent, and apparent clothing until your next rest. You cannot perfectly duplicate a specific person without close study.
- **Not Quite Here:** Once per rest when a mundane restraint, barrier, or environmental hazard would trap you, mark a Stress to slip the constraint in an uncanny but plausible way.

### Revenant
You died. Something about that statement failed to remain final.
- **Dead Memory:** You do not need to breathe and have advantage against ordinary poison, disease, and environmental suffocation.
- **Refuse the Ending:** Once per long rest when you mark your last HP, mark a Stress to remain conscious long enough to take one immediate move before resolving your normal death move.

### Bloodbound
Your lineage carries an inherited predatory supernatural trait.
- **Predator's Senses:** You have advantage when tracking a nearby creature by blood, heartbeat, or scent.
- **Hunger:** Once per rest after you deal Severe damage to a living adversary at Very Close range, clear a Stress. If you do, the GM describes one visible sign of your hunger.

### Spirit-Touched
A spirit, place, ancestor, or impossible event changed what you are.
- **Resonance:** Choose storm, flame, shadow, river, stone, machine, dream, or grave. You automatically notice a strong nearby supernatural manifestation of that resonance.
- **Manifestation:** Once per rest, spend a Hope to create a brief minor supernatural effect consistent with your resonance. It can solve a small narrative obstacle but cannot duplicate a major domain-card effect.

### Constructed
Your body or soul was rebuilt through occult engineering, alchemy, impossible surgery, enchanted machinery, or a deliberate act of creation.
- **Integrated Tool:** Choose one mundane tool or device physically integrated into you. You always count as having access to it when your body is functional.
- **Hard to Kill:** When you take Severe damage, you may mark an Armor Slot without reducing that damage to clear a Stress.

## Communities

### Streetborne
Neighborhood networks, informal authority, survival knowledge, and local relationships shaped you.
- **I Know Someone:** Once per session, name a plausible local contact who can provide a lead, routine access, or a small favor. The GM says what they need in return.

### Institutionborne
You were shaped by medicine, law, military service, academia, emergency response, journalism, government, or another formal institution.
- **Credentials:** Gain advantage when legitimate professional standing would ordinarily grant routine access, cooperation, records, or credibility.

### Occultborne
You grew up inside the hidden world.
- **Old Etiquette:** The first time you deal with an established supernatural faction, ask the GM what taboo, courtesy, or obligation matters most. Gain a Hope when you knowingly respect it at meaningful personal cost.

### Networkborne
Online communities, hackerspaces, activist networks, makers, gig work, and distributed relationships shaped you more than one neighborhood.
- **Signal Boost:** Once per rest, when digital research or distributed contacts could plausibly help, spend a Hope to obtain one useful lead without a roll. The GM may attach a complication.

### Sanctuaryborne
A church, mutual-aid house, occult safehouse, protected enclave, or hidden neighborhood taught you that survival is communal.
- **Shelter:** Once per session, identify a plausible temporary safe place. It remains safe until you violate its rules or bring overwhelming attention to it.

### Courtborne
You grew up around old supernatural status, hidden wealth, inherited influence, or formal occult politics.
- **Protocol:** Gain advantage when identifying status, etiquette, precedent, or leverage within a formal supernatural hierarchy.

### Corporateborne
You learned how organizations hide responsibility behind process, ownership, and plausible deniability.
- **Paper Trail:** Once per session, when investigating a company or institution, ask the GM which mundane record category—ownership, contracts, payroll, insurance, litigation, procurement, or property—offers the best next lead.

### Faithborne
A religious, contemplative, or spiritual community taught you practices for meaning, discipline, service, and confronting the inexplicable.
- **Ritual Grounding:** Once per rest when you take a quiet moment to perform a personally meaningful practice, clear a Stress or give an ally within Very Close range advantage on their next reaction roll against supernatural fear or influence.

# Veiled City Domain Access
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE • PLAYTEST HOMEBREW**

The four Veiled City domains are additions to the SRD domain ecosystem.

A PC may replace one of their class's two normal domains at character creation with one Veiled City domain. This is a campaign-frame rule, not a change to the core Daggerheart class.

| Domain | Identity | Typical play |
|---|---|---|
| Hex | curses, names, sympathetic links, circles, bindings | control, investigation, ritual leverage |
| Signal | technology, networks, machine spirits, surveillance, electricity | information, ranged utility, systems control |
| Veil | concealment, perception, thresholds, memory, secrecy | infiltration, defense, social/scene control |
| Pact | spirits, patrons, favors, promises, borrowed power | negotiation, flexible support, costly power |

All four custom domains are playtest material. If a combination produces a loop that repeatedly generates resources without meaningful cost or bypasses a class's intended limitation, the AI GM should flag the interaction and make a provisional ruling rather than silently exploiting or banning it.

# Occult Scars
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE once acquired • GM may keep unoffered cards private**

Occult Scars are optional Veiled City campaign cards representing lasting supernatural changes. They are **not** ancestries and do not replace official Daggerheart transformations. A character normally gains one only through play and explicit player consent.

## Ghostbound
You survived contact with the dead badly enough that part of the boundary stayed open.
- **Gift — Cold Door:** Once per rest, spend a Hope to see and hear nearby ghosts or strong psychic residues for a scene.
- **Price — Thin Presence:** On a Fear result while using the Gift, mundane electronics around you briefly malfunction and Veil Exposure may rise if witnesses are present.

## Moonbound
A predatory lunar spirit, curse, or lineage has begun rewriting your instincts.
- **Gift — Hunt:** Once per rest, mark a Stress to gain advantage while tracking a creature you have personally encountered.
- **Price — Instinct:** When you are badly injured or cornered, the GM may offer a Hope to act on a predatory impulse. You may always refuse without penalty.

## Blood-Cursed
Your vitality has become entangled with blood magic.
- **Gift — Red Recovery:** Once per long rest after you cause a hostile living adversary to mark HP at Melee range, clear 1 HP or 2 Stress.
- **Price — Invitation:** Certain blood-working entities can recognize you on sight and may treat you as part of their jurisdiction.

## Hollow Passenger
Something shares space with you, willingly or otherwise.
- **Gift — Second Voice:** Once per rest, ask your Passenger a question about supernatural danger, another entity, or a hidden motive. The GM answers according to what the Passenger knows.
- **Price — Agenda:** The Passenger has a concrete want recorded in GM state. It cannot seize your PC without consent, but it can bargain, withhold cooperation, or create complications consistent with established rules.

## Glitch-Touched
Your supernatural presence has fused with the informational world.
- **Gift — Impossible Login:** Once per rest, spend a Hope to authenticate to one mundane electronic system as though you possessed ordinary authorized credentials, provided the system is networked and not specifically warded against you.
- **Price — Artifact:** Your supernatural actions sometimes leave repeating digital signatures that skilled occult investigators can recognize.

## Sainted
A community, spirit, miracle, or act of sacrifice has given your presence symbolic weight.
- **Gift — Intercession:** Once per long rest when another creature within Close range would mark their last HP, spend 2 Hope to let them remain at 1 unmarked HP instead.
- **Price — Expectation:** People and entities who recognize what you have become may ask things of you that they would not ask of an ordinary person.

# Hex Domain
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE • PLAYTEST HOMEBREW**

Curses, names, sympathetic links, circles, bindings, and deliberate magical consequences. Design threads: **leverage through connection** and **control through ritual rules**.

This domain contains 21 cards: three Level 1 cards and two cards at each level from 2–10.

## Level 1

### Sympathetic Thread
**Spell • Recall Cost 0**
When you hold a meaningful physical link to a target, spend a Hope and make a Spellcast Roll. On a success, while the link remains in your possession you can sense the target’s rough direction while they are within the same district. On a success with Fear, the target senses that something is reaching for them.

### Jinx
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Make a Spellcast Roll against an adversary within Close range. On a success, they are **Vulnerable** until they succeed on an action against you or an ally. Once per target per scene.

### Salt Circle
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
During a moment of preparation, mark a Stress to establish a protective circle within Very Close range. Minor supernatural creatures cannot willingly cross it unless the circle is physically broken or they succeed on a Reaction Roll against your Spellcast result.

## Level 2

### Hexsense
**Ability • Recall Cost 0**
When you closely examine a person, object, or place, you can tell whether it is currently cursed, bound by an oath, carrying a sympathetic link, or serving as part of a ritual. Spend a Hope to ask the GM which of those categories is most significant.

### Crooked Fortune
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
When an adversary within Close range succeeds on an attack against you, you can mark a Stress to give the damage roll disadvantage. Describe the small coincidence that turns the blow.

## Level 3

### Pin the Shadow
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Make a Spellcast Roll against a target within Close range. On a success, the target is **Restrained** by its own shadow until it succeeds on a Reaction Roll (14) or the lighting changes dramatically.

### Countercurse
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
When you touch a cursed target, make a Spellcast Roll against the curse’s Difficulty. On a success, suppress one curse effect until your next rest. Spend a Hope to end a minor curse entirely.

## Level 4

### Name's Weight
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
When you have learned a target’s true supernatural name, sworn title, or equivalent intimate identifier, record it. Once per scene when directly opposing that target, you can spend a Hope to gain advantage and add your Knowledge to the result.

### Mirror the Malice
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
When a supernatural effect would impose a condition on you, mark a Stress and make a Spellcast Roll against its source. On a success, you still suffer the effect, but the source becomes **Vulnerable** until the end of its next spotlight.

## Level 5

### Binding Clause
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
You and a willing creature state a clear promise and consequence, then make a Spellcast Roll (15). On a success, the bargain becomes magically witnessed. A knowing breach immediately reveals itself to both parties and triggers the agreed narrative consequence if it is within the scale of the promise.

### Black Thread
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
After you witness a creature knowingly break a promise, spend a Hope to mark them with a Black Thread until your next long rest. You always know whether they are within Far range, and your first successful attack or Spellcast Roll against them in a scene deals an extra d8 damage or gives them disadvantage on their next action.

## Level 6

### Break the Working
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Make a Spellcast Roll against a persistent magical effect within Close range. On a success, end or dismantle one ongoing spell, ward, curse, or ritual of your tier or lower. More powerful workings may instead lose one major protection or requirement, as the GM describes.

### Witch's Ladder
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
During a long rest, bind a single emotion or simple sensory impression into a small object. Later, crush or untie it to release that impression strongly within Very Close range. You can have a number of ladders equal to your Proficiency; old ones unravel if you exceed the limit.

## Level 7

### Hex-Touched
**Ability • Recall Cost 2**
When at least four cards in your loadout are from Hex, gain +1 Knowledge. Once per rest, when a creature succeeds on a Reaction Roll against one of your Hex cards, you can mark a Stress to force them to reroll it.

### Stolen Fortune
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Once per rest, when a target within Close range critically succeeds or succeeds with Hope, spend 2 Hope to steal the bright edge of the moment. Their result remains a success, but treat it as a success with Fear for narrative consequence, and you clear a Stress.

## Level 8

### Generational Curse
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
With a personal link and at least an hour of ritual work, define a lasting curse with the GM. It must include a concrete trigger, visible symptom, meaningful escape condition, and reciprocal vulnerability or cost to you. Powerful targets may require additional leverage before the curse can take hold.

### Circle Without Door
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Make a Spellcast Roll (17) to seal a Close-range boundary you can physically trace. On a success, one named supernatural target inside cannot cross the boundary until it succeeds on a Reaction Roll (17), the circle is broken from outside, or you leave Far range.

## Level 9

### Sever the Name
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, make a Spellcast Roll against an adversary whose true supernatural name you know. On a success, choose one non-passive feature the GM has revealed. The adversary cannot use that feature until your next rest or until it fulfills a meaningful condition the GM states.

### Doom Mark
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Spend a Hope and make a Spellcast Roll against a target within Far range. On a success, mark them until the end of the scene. The first time each round they take Severe damage, they mark 1 additional HP; then you mark a Stress.

## Level 10

### Rewrite the Bargain
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, when you can physically access a supernatural contract, geas, or witnessed pact, make a Spellcast Roll against its power. On a success, alter one clause without nullifying the agreement. The new clause must preserve equivalent obligation; the GM tells you what force resists or notices the edit.

### Word of Unmaking
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, spend 3 Hope and name a magical object, ward, summoned construct, or supernatural manifestation within Close range. Make a Spellcast Roll (20). On a success, either destroy a non-legendary effect outright or force a legendary target to mark 4 HP and lose one defensive feature until the end of the scene.

# Signal Domain
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE • PLAYTEST HOMEBREW**

Technology, networks, machine spirits, surveillance, infrastructure, and information. Design threads: **know the system** and **become part of the system**.

This domain contains 21 cards: three Level 1 cards and two cards at each level from 2–10.

## Level 1

### Ghost in the Machine
**Spell • Recall Cost 0**
Touch or connect to an electronic device and make a Spellcast Roll. On a success, communicate briefly with the residual personality of the system and ask one question about its recent use, errors, inputs, or observations. It answers according to what the device could actually know.

### Trace Route
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
When examining a message, recording, transaction, or digital artifact, spend a Hope to identify one useful connection: likely source, destination, alteration, hidden recipient, unusual gap, or repeated identifier.

### Live Feed
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Choose a functioning camera, microphone, sensor, or connected device within Close range that you can see or identify. Until you leave Far range, you may perceive through it as though standing beside it. Mark a Stress to jump to another linked device.

## Level 2

### Dead Zone
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Make a Spellcast Roll (13). On a success, ordinary wireless communication and casual recording fail within Close range until you dismiss the effect or leave. Hardened systems and supernatural communications may require a separate roll to suppress.

### Pattern Recognition
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
After reviewing at least three related pieces of evidence, ask the GM one question about a recurring pattern, shared source, timing relationship, or meaningful anomaly. The GM answers truthfully; the answer may identify what additional evidence is needed.

## Level 3

### Augmented Awareness
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
While you have legitimate or hacked access to functioning local cameras or sensors, treat their coverage as an extension of your senses for noticing movement and obvious events. This does not automatically defeat Veil, Hex, or magical concealment.

### Static Arc
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Make a Spellcast Roll against a target within Close range. On a success, deal d8+3 magic damage using your Proficiency and push the target to Very Close range. Against an electronic or machine-spirit target, add a d6 to the damage roll.

## Level 4

### Burner Identity
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
During a rest, establish a temporary mundane digital identity with believable surface records. It survives routine automated checks until the next long rest. Deep human scrutiny, biometric conflict, or privileged databases can expose it.

### Backdoor
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Make a Spellcast Roll against a networked system you can access. On a success, establish a hidden route that gives you advantage on your next three rolls to enter, control, or extract information from that system. On Fear, leave a detectable anomaly.

## Level 5

### Viral Haunting
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Imprint a minor supernatural phenomenon into a digital file you control. When willingly opened, the file can deliver a message, sensory manifestation, tracking ping, or one-use Signal effect you specify. It travels only through deliberate copying or sharing.

### Predictive Model
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
Once per rest after studying a person, route, or system, spend a Hope and ask what it is most likely to do next if no one interferes. The GM gives the most probable near-term behavior based on current information, not a guaranteed future.

## Level 6

### Infrastructure Spirit
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
At a major piece of infrastructure, spend a Hope and call its local spirit. Make a Spellcast Roll. On a success, it manifests enough to bargain for information or one substantial service appropriate to the system: rerouting access, opening a path, locating an interruption, or hiding a trace.

### Hard Reset
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Make a Spellcast Roll against a technological or Signal-based adversary within Far range. On a success, it loses its next special Action feature and becomes **Vulnerable** until it next takes the spotlight. A living target with implants instead chooses one effect.

## Level 7

### Signal-Touched
**Ability • Recall Cost 2**
When at least four cards in your loadout are from Signal, gain +1 Knowledge. Once per rest, when you roll with Fear while using technology or Signal magic, you may mark a Stress to treat the result as though the Fear die did not generate an additional GM consequence.

### Ghost Network
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
During a rest, bind up to your Proficiency willing creatures into a private supernatural communications channel until the next rest. Members can exchange brief thoughts or speech while within the same district, but powerful wards may block the connection.

## Level 8

### Blackout
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per rest, make a Spellcast Roll (17). On a success, choose a building or Very Far area. For the scene, ordinary powered systems fail or enter safe mode unless you exempt them. Supernatural and hardened systems make a Reaction Roll (17) to remain active.

### Machine Possession
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Make a Spellcast Roll to enter a willing, abandoned, or defeated machine or vehicle within Close range. Until you leave it or take Severe damage, perceive through it and operate its ordinary functions remotely. Your body is **Vulnerable** while your attention is inside.

## Level 9

### City as Circuit
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, spend 2 Hope while connected to local infrastructure. Ask the GM three questions about movement, power, surveillance, communications, or active supernatural disturbances in the surrounding district. Receive truthful useful answers limited by what the city’s systems can sense.

### Quantum Alibi
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
When evidence would conclusively place you or an ally at a supernatural incident, spend a Hope and make a Spellcast Roll (19). On a success, create a plausible contradictory digital trail. It does not erase physical evidence and may itself become suspicious under expert review.

## Level 10

### Kill Switch
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, make a Spellcast Roll against all technological or Signal-based adversaries within Far range. Targets you succeed against mark 3 HP and cannot use special Actions until after the GM next gains Fear.

### Everywhere at Once
**Ability • Recall Cost 3**
While you are inside a heavily networked district, spend 3 Hope to become present through its connected devices for one scene. You may speak, perceive, and originate Signal spells from any accessible local device. Your physical body remains where you left it and is **Vulnerable**.

# Veil Domain
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE • PLAYTEST HOMEBREW**

Concealment, thresholds, perception, memory, secrecy, and reality’s preference for mundane explanations. Design threads: **avoid notice** and **control boundaries**.

This domain contains 21 cards: three Level 1 cards and two cards at each level from 2–10.

## Level 1

### Look Away
**Spell • Recall Cost 0**
Spend a Hope to make one small supernatural detail seem mundane to casual observers until the end of the scene: strange eyes, a ritual mark, an impossible object, or similar. Deliberate investigation or contradictory evidence can break the effect.

### Read the Room
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
When you enter a social scene, ask the GM which visible person is most frightened, most dangerous, or most out of place. The GM answers one. Once per scene.

### Threshold Sense
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
You automatically notice magically significant thresholds, invitations, wards, sanctuaries, and places where the Veil is unusually thin when you come within Very Close range of them.

## Level 2

### Borrowed Face
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Make a Spellcast Roll (13) to alter your apparent face, voice, hair, and clothing until your next rest. You can resemble a type of person or an invented identity, but perfect imitation of a specific individual requires having studied them closely.

### Blur Evidence
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
After a supernatural event within Close range, mark a Stress to cause casual recordings and eyewitness memories to become ambiguous. This cannot erase a clear physical trace or defeat deliberate forensic work; it buys uncertainty.

## Level 3

### Sanctuary Line
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Trace a threshold and name who is welcome. Until you leave Far range, creatures not included must succeed on a Reaction Roll against your Spellcast result to cross it without becoming **Vulnerable** and immediately revealing their presence to you.

### Blind Spot
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Make a Spellcast Roll (14). On a success, create a Very Close area that casual observation skips over until you move or attack. Anyone specifically searching the area can make an Instinct Reaction Roll against your Spellcast result to notice it.

## Level 4

### Missing Minute
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
After a brief supernatural event, make a Spellcast Roll against nearby casual witnesses. On a success, they remember the event as confused fragments and ordinary explanations. You cannot erase deeply personal memories, trained observation, or durable recordings.

### Door Between
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
When two mundane thresholds within Close range are both unobserved, mark a Stress and make a Spellcast Roll (15). On a success, step through one and emerge from the other. You cannot carry an unwilling creature through.

## Level 5

### Hidden Room
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Spend a Hope and spend several minutes establishing a ward around a small room or vehicle. Until your next long rest, ordinary searches overlook it unless investigators possess a specific reason to examine that exact place or use supernatural means.

### Mirror Double
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Create a convincing visual and audible double within Close range until you take Severe damage or dismiss it. When an adversary attacks you, you can mark a Stress to move the double and give that attack disadvantage.

## Level 6

### Nobody Here
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
When a scene begins and no hostile creature is directly observing you, spend a Hope. Until you make an attack, cast an obvious spell, or deliberately draw attention, ordinary bystanders treat you as irrelevant background. Important NPCs can resist with an Instinct Reaction Roll (16).

### Protected Witness
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
Choose one willing person during a rest. Until your next long rest, supernatural attempts to locate them through names, dreams, scrying, or sympathetic links have disadvantage, and you immediately sense when such an attempt occurs.

## Level 7

### Veil-Touched
**Ability • Recall Cost 2**
When at least four cards in your loadout are from Veil, gain +1 Presence. Once per rest, when an adversary succeeds on an attack because it detected or located you, you can mark a Stress to force it to reroll the attack.

### False History
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
During a long rest, construct a coherent false supernatural identity for yourself. Until your next long rest, divinations and casual occult inquiries return that identity unless their source succeeds on a Reaction Roll (17). Mundane records are unchanged.

## Level 8

### Walk Between Notice
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per rest, spend a Hope to become functionally absent from casual perception while moving through a populated area. Direct hostile attention, physical obstruction, or overt violence ends the effect. Cameras record you but casual review tends to skip the footage.

### Seal the Threshold
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Make a Spellcast Roll (18) while touching a doorway, gate, road, bridge, or other meaningful boundary. On a success, choose a named supernatural category or creature. It cannot cross for the scene without succeeding on a Reaction Roll (18) and marking 2 Stress.

## Level 9

### Local Rewrite
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, spend 2 Hope and define one plausible mundane explanation for a supernatural event that occurred this scene. Make a Spellcast Roll (19). On a success, the Veil strongly favors that explanation among uninvolved witnesses and institutions. Physical evidence that directly disproves it remains dangerous.

### Memory Palace
**Ability • Recall Cost 2**
You can partition your mind into protected rooms. Gain advantage on reaction rolls against memory alteration, possession, or coercive supernatural influence. Once per rest when such an effect succeeds anyway, spend a Hope to preserve one chosen fact untouched.

## Level 10

### Perfect Disappearance
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, spend 3 Hope and name yourself or one willing creature you touch. For the scene, supernatural tracking fails, casual witnesses lose the thread of pursuit, and ordinary systems stop flagging the target in real time. Existing records remain.

### Veil Sovereign
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Make a Spellcast Roll (20) to command the local Veil within Far range for one scene. Choose two: conceal supernatural activity from casual witnesses; reveal all active magical concealments to you; give allies advantage to hide or escape; force supernatural infiltrators to reveal their true outlines. Afterward, increase Veil Exposure by 1 unless you accept a major GM consequence.

# Pact Domain
**Visibility: PLAYER SAFE • PLAYTEST HOMEBREW**

Spirits, patrons, favors, promises, obligations, and power borrowed through relationships. Design threads: **negotiated flexibility** and **power with explicit cost**.

This domain contains 21 cards: three Level 1 cards and two cards at each level from 2–10.

## Level 1

### Minor Favor
**Spell • Recall Cost 0**
Name a known spirit, ancestor, patron, or supernatural contact and request a small service. Spend a Hope. The GM states the price before you accept. If no plausible entity could answer, the Hope is not spent.

### Oathmark
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
When you willingly promise a concrete service to another creature and they clearly accept, you may take a Hope. Until you fulfill the promise, mark a Stress whenever you knowingly make fulfillment substantially harder. You can maintain only one Oathmark at a time.

### Call the Witness
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Make a Spellcast Roll to invite a nearby minor spirit to manifest enough to converse. It is not compelled to cooperate, but it must identify what kind of offering, courtesy, or leverage would interest it.

## Level 2

### Borrowed Aspect
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Invoke a known patron or spirit and spend a Hope to gain one minor narrative capability consistent with its nature until the end of the scene, such as seeing in smoke, walking on shallow water, or speaking through mirrors. It cannot duplicate a higher-level card.

### Price Named
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
When an NPC or supernatural entity offers a bargain, ask the GM what the offer truly costs in obligation, risk, or loss if your character could reasonably understand the terms. The answer is truthful, though unknowable future consequences remain unknowable.

## Level 3

### Familiar Covenant
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
During a long rest, form a covenant with a willing minor spirit. Give it a name, nature, want, and taboo. Once per rest it can scout within Far range or grant advantage on one action aligned with its nature. If you violate its taboo, the covenant suspends until you make amends.

### Take My Hand
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
When an ally within Very Close range would mark Stress, you can mark that Stress instead. If this fills your last Stress slot, gain a Hope.

## Level 4

### Favor Bank
**Ability • Recall Cost 1**
When you substantially help a named supernatural NPC without immediate payment, place a Favor beside their name in campaign state. Once per session, spend one Favor to obtain plausible access, information, shelter, or assistance from them; the GM decides the exact form.

### Spirit Road
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Spend a Hope and make a Spellcast Roll (15) at a meaningful threshold. On a success, open a brief route through the spirit world to another threshold you have personally visited within the same district. Everyone who crosses risks attracting local attention on a Fear result.

## Level 5

### Collect the Debt
**Ability • Recall Cost 2**
Once per session, call in a genuine favor or obligation owed by an NPC or supernatural entity. They must provide meaningful assistance within the scope of the debt, but retain agency over how they fulfill it.

### Vessel
**Spell • Recall Cost 1**
Invite a willing minor spirit into yourself for one scene. Choose one benefit consistent with it: +2 Evasion, +2 to one trait, or add a d8 to magic damage rolls. The GM names one visible tell and one impulse you feel while hosting it.

## Level 6

### Greater Invocation
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Call on a powerful known entity for a major effect. State what you want; before you commit, the GM states the demanded price and a likely collateral risk. If you accept, make a Spellcast Roll appropriate to the scale. On a failure, the price is not automatically waived.

### Intercession
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
When you or an ally within Close range would mark 2 or more HP, spend 2 Hope and invoke a pact-holder who could plausibly intervene. Reduce the severity by one threshold, then the GM introduces a visible sign of the entity’s attention.

## Level 7

### Pact-Touched
**Ability • Recall Cost 2**
When at least four cards in your loadout are from Pact, gain +1 Presence. Once per rest when you pay a cost explicitly demanded by a supernatural bargain, gain a Hope or clear a Stress.

### Renegotiate
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
When a supernatural entity invokes an obligation you genuinely owe, make a Presence Roll against it. On a success, propose an alternative service of comparable weight. The entity must either accept it or reveal what additional concession would make it acceptable.

## Level 8

### Patron's Emissary
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per rest, spend a Hope to summon or manifest an emissary of a known powerful entity within Close range. It remains for the scene and can perform one significant task aligned with its patron before departing. The GM controls its personality and limits.

### Shared Fate
**Spell • Recall Cost 2**
Choose a willing ally within Close range and make a Spellcast Roll (17). On a success, until the scene ends, either of you may mark 1 Stress to take HP that the other would mark from an attack. Each transfer must be chosen before Armor is used.

## Level 9

### Covenant
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Forge a lasting pact with a major supernatural power. Define with the GM one significant boon, one binding obligation, and one prohibition. All terms become campaign canon. The boon must be comparable to a high-level domain feature, not unrestricted wish fulfillment.

### Authority of Witness
**Ability • Recall Cost 2**
When you personally witness a supernatural being knowingly break a sworn pact, spend 2 Hope. For the rest of the scene, you have advantage on all actions directly opposing that being and it cannot benefit from magical concealment against you.

## Level 10

### Call In Everything
**Ability • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, spend all remaining Hope (minimum 2) and name up to your Proficiency debts, favors, pacts, or allies that could plausibly affect the current crisis. The GM brings their combined influence into the scene in a powerful but fictionally appropriate way. Every invoked relationship changes afterward.

### Become the Clause
**Spell • Recall Cost 3**
Once per long rest, make a Spellcast Roll (20) while invoking a pact you are party to. On a success, embody one clearly stated principle of that pact for the scene—protection, pursuit, silence, passage, vengeance, or another agreed principle. Gain advantage on actions that directly enact it and resistance to effects that directly contradict it. When the scene ends, mark 2 Stress.
