# Rent Due in Blood
**Visibility: GM_PRIVATE / AI DM ONLY — FULL MYSTERY SPOILERS**
**Tier 1 • Threshold Mystery / Housing / Predatory Contract Magic**

## Hook
Residents of **Bellweather Arms**, an aging apartment building, begin receiving eviction notices for impossible lease violations. One notice is addressed to **Marian Bell**, who died in the building in 1994. Another tenant wakes outside after dreaming that the building “revoked permission” for them to be inside.

## Fixed hidden truth
The building’s new owner, **Halcyon Residential**, bought an old Pale Court property portfolio. Buried in the title documents is a supernatural hospitality covenant: the recognized owner can revoke residence from occupants who violate specified house rules.

A property manager discovered fragments of the covenant through an occult consultant and is using it to accelerate evictions before redevelopment. The consultant did not understand that the covenant treats long-term tenants, not the landlord, as part of the building’s “household.” The magic is becoming unstable because ownership and belonging now conflict.

Marian Bell’s ghost is not the attacker. She is trying to warn tenants because she helped organize the building decades ago and was named as a household witness in an old tenant agreement.

## Clock
0. Strange notices.
1. Doors fail to open for selected tenants.
2. Building systems selectively reject residents.
3. One tenant is physically expelled by threshold force.
4. Property management brings private security and locksmiths.
5. The covenant manifests a hostile **House Bailiff** (use Glass Market Knife stats with magic damage and threshold movement).
6. The building attempts to “clear the household” during a mass lockout.

## Core clues
1. All affected tenants are in units targeted for renovation.
2. Eviction language contains archaic wording absent from the current lease.
3. Halcyon’s title chain includes a 1912 ownership transfer witnessed by a Pale Court notary.
4. Marian Bell’s old tenant association minutes use the same phrase “household in good standing.”
5. Threshold Sense or occult investigation shows doors responding to belonging, not legal tenancy alone.
6. A maintenance worker saw the property manager drawing symbols inside doorframes.
7. The consultant’s invoice refers to “revocation protocol” and “legacy covenant activation.”
8. The property manager believes the ritual is only intimidation and does not know it can kill someone.

## Major scenes
- **Tenant meeting:** establish people with real mundane stakes. Give the PC reasons to care beyond “haunting.”
- **Title search:** public archive, Samir Patel, or Nia Brooks can reveal property history.
- **Night lockout:** use **Apartment Veil Breach**; several residents are trapped outside or inside.
- **Marian’s testimony:** at a meaningful threshold, her ghost can explain how the old household agreement worked.
- **Final remedy:** legal filing, ritual redefinition of household, negotiated Court precedent, destruction of the covenant, or confrontation with management.

## Resolutions
**Redefine the household:** strongest restorative ending. Tenants collectively become the covenant’s protected household; ownership no longer grants unilateral magical eviction.

**Destroy the covenant:** ends magic but may weaken a minor local threshold and attract opportunistic entities later.

**Court arbitration:** Concord/Pale Court can enforce precedent, but this places the building inside supernatural politics.

**Mundane victory only:** a court injunction can stop eviction but leaves unstable threshold magic requiring a later fix.
