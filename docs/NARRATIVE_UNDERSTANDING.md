# Conversational meaning is not campaign authority

The GM can interpret ordinary contextual names, pronouns and follow-up references
without treating them as state changes. The coarse player-language classifier's
`unclear` result does not itself require a clarification question. Questions should
be reserved for unresolved ambiguity that materially affects the proposed action.

Entry declarations still stage a native pending review, but no longer intercept
the conversation with a compulsory “which exact place?” response. A bounded,
owner/source/session/scene-scoped reference packet preserves pending destination
names alongside the saved location. Private attempts are excluded from party-turn
reference packets. These are interpretive context, not arrival evidence, access
permission, encounter resolution, presence, NPC knowledge, consent or canon.

Acknowledging a clarification normally needs no consequential narrative claim.
The generation contract and bounded correction retry make this distinction explicit.
A narrow compatibility check also accepts the reported redundant `intent` movement
claim on “That clarifies the destination of your earlier entry attempt.” Only an
active, authenticated, same-session owner declaration visible to every actual output
audience qualifies. The exception is anchored to the entire acknowledgement sentence;
it does not allow appended travel assertions, damage, disclosure or committed movement.

No structured mutation authority is granted by conversational context or acknowledgement.
Native human entry review still requires an established explicitly named location,
a current pending revision, an active owner source, unchanged session/scene/location,
and no active encounter. Human adjudication of access remains required; pending
restricted or unknown places cannot become accessible merely through prose.
Existing privacy, committed-position and witness checks remain in place.

Offline regressions exercise natural-language follow-ups through the GM service,
first-pass acknowledgement, pending review preservation, private/party isolation,
unknown/retracted/foreign sources, committed-arrival refusal, hidden occupants,
and encounter review. They establish bounded fixture behavior, not unrestricted
live-model language understanding. No extra interpretation model call is added;
normal GM generation handles the conversation after the router stages review.

No new flags or delegations are required. The existing `natural_language` and
`scene_continuity` flags govern entry reference packets and review staging.
